# Episode 6: えきで しつもん — Where Is the Toilet? Asking Questions at the Station
*2026-11-23 · Topic: asking for directions at a station · Audio: `2026-11-23_ep006.mp3`*

## Today's pattern

### Aは なん／どこ／だれ ですか  ·  *A wa nan / doko / dare desu ka*  =  **What / where / who is A?**

- **A**: the topic you're asking about: a thing, place, or person (トイレ, あれ, このかさ, えき)
- **question word**: なん (what), どこ (where), or だれ (who). It goes exactly where the answer will go. Add の for 'whose' (だれの) or 'what kind of' (なんの)

**なん／どこ／だれ** — Question words slot in where the answer goes

Structure: `[Topic] は なん ですか。`

### Building practical sentences

1. トイレ  *toire*  → toilet
2. トイレは  *toire wa*  → as for the toilet
3. トイレはどこ  *toire wa doko*  → as for the toilet, where
4. トイレはどこですか。  *toire wa doko desu ka.*  → Where is the toilet?

1. あれ  *are*  → that over there
2. あれは  *are wa*  → as for that over there
3. あれはなん  *are wa nan*  → as for that over there, what
4. あれはなんですか。  *are wa nan desu ka.*  → What is that over there?

1. このかさ  *kono kasa*  → this umbrella
2. このかさは  *kono kasa wa*  → as for this umbrella
3. このかさはだれのかさ  *kono kasa wa dare no kasa*  → as for this umbrella, whose umbrella
4. このかさはだれのかさですか。  *kono kasa wa dare no kasa desu ka.*  → Whose umbrella is this?

> **Tip:** Don't move the question word to the front like in English. Put it right where the answer will go: トイレはどこですか → トイレはあれです. Also, "what" is なに, but before です it becomes なん: say なんですか, never なにですか.

### Real Japanese (TV quiz show host)

**では、もんだいです。このどうぶつはなんですか。**  
Okay, here's the question. What is this animal?

In Japanese, you don't move words around to make a question. Start with an ordinary statement like トイレは あれです (The toilet is that one). Put a question word (どこ where, 何 what, だれ who) in the slot where the answer would go, then add か at the end. That gives トイレは どこですか (Where is the toilet?). The answer then uses the same frame, with the real information in that slot.

- Frame: [Topic] は [question word] です か。 The question word sits right before です, in the same slot the answer noun will fill.
- English moves 'where' to the front of the sentence. Japanese leaves it in the answer's position: トイレは どこですか → トイレは あれです.
- 何 is read なん before です and before の: 何ですか (nan desu ka), 何の みせ (nan no mise, 'what kind of shop').
- A question word can also join the の pattern: だれの かさ = 'who's umbrella' → 'whose umbrella'. 何の みせ = 'shop of what' → 'what kind of shop'.
- か still marks the question, just like in yes/no questions. The difference is that the answer is information, not はい or いいえ.

*This is the 〜ですか yes/no question you already know, with a question word in the noun slot. It also combines with の (だれの かさ) and with これ/それ/あれ (あれは 何ですか).*

### Swap-in examples

1. えきはどこですか。  
   *Eki wa doko desu ka.*  
   Where is the station?  (swap → topic: トイレ → えき)

2. でぐちはどこですか。  
   *Deguchi wa doko desu ka.*  
   Where is the exit?  (swap → topic: えき → でぐち)

3. それは何(なん)ですか。  
   *Sore wa nan desu ka.*  
   What is that?  (swap → question word: どこ → 何 (topic: でぐち → それ))

4. せんせいはだれですか。  
   *Sensei wa dare desu ka.*  
   Who is the teacher?  (swap → question word: 何 → だれ (topic: それ → せんせい))

5. このメニューはだれのメニューですか。  
   *Kono menyuu wa dare no menyuu desu ka.*  
   Whose menu is this?  (swap → answer slot: だれ → だれの メニュー (topic: この メニュー))

**1. Yuki:** すみません、トイレはどこですか。

## Teacher script

Welcome back! Today's pattern is **Aはなんですか**. It means: What is A?  
And it has two cousins: **Aはどこですか**, Where is A? And **Aはだれですか**, Who is A?  
Here's the big secret. Japanese doesn't move question words to the front like English does. The question word sits right where the answer will go.  
You already know **AはBです**, A is B. To ask a question, just drop a question word into the B slot and finish with **ですか**.  
So: **なん** means what. **どこ** means where. **だれ** means who. And **ですか** still comes last, turning it into a question.  
Let's build one you'll really need. Start with the topic.  
> 🔊 トイレ  
Toilet. Now add the topic marker **は**.  
> 🔊 トイレは  
As for the toilet. Now put the question word in the answer slot.  
> 🔊 トイレはどこ  
As for the toilet, where. Finish with **ですか**.  
> 🔊 トイレはどこですか。  
Where is the toilet? And the answer simply replaces **どこ**:  
> 🔊 トイレはあれです。  
The toilet is that one over there. Same skeleton, answer dropped into the same slot.  
Second one. Start with that thing over there, **あれ**, add **は**, then **なん**.  
> 🔊 あれは  
> 🔊 あれはなん  
> 🔊 あれはなんですか。  
What is that over there?  
Third. Start with **このかさ**, this umbrella. Add **は**.  
> 🔊 このかさは  
As for this umbrella. Now remember **の**, the word for 's? Stick it on **だれ**, and **だれの** means whose.  
> 🔊 このかさはだれのかさ  
> 🔊 このかさはだれのかさですか。  
Whose umbrella is this? The answer, again, slides into that slot:  
> 🔊 それは私のかさです。  
That's my umbrella.  
Now a swap. Keep the skeleton, change the topic.  
> 🔊 えきはどこですか。  
Where is the station?  
> 🔊 でぐちはどこですか。  
Where is the exit? One pattern, endless questions.  
A common mistake: the word for what is **なに**, but right before **です** it shortens to **なん**. So say **なんですか**, never **なにですか**.  
And **なん** plus **の** gives **なんの**, what kind of. Like this:  
> 🔊 でぐちのみせはなんのみせですか。  
What kind of shop is the shop at the exit?  
So today's piece snaps right onto everything you know: **は** and **です** from day one, **か** for questions, **の** for whose, and **これ**, **それ**, **あれ** for your answers.
