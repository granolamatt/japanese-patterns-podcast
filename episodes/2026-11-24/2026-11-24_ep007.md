# Episode 7: しゅうまつの よてい — Weekend Plans — "Me Too!" with も
*2026-11-24 · Topic: weekend plans · Audio: `2026-11-24_ep007.mp3`*

## Today's pattern

### AもBです  ·  *A mo B desu*  =  **A is also B / A is B, too**

- **A**: The topic that is 'also' true: a person (わたし, ゆき), a thing, a day (にちようび), or a の-chunk like わたしのよてい. も replaces は here.
- **B**: A noun that was already said about something else: a day off (やすみ), a movie (えいが), a place (えき).

**〜も** — も = also / too (replaces は)

Structure: `[Topic] も [Noun] です。`

### Building practical sentences

1. わたし  *watashi*  → I
2. わたしも  *watashi mo*  → me too / I also
3. わたしもやすみです。  *watashi mo yasumi desu.*  → I'm off too.

1. わたしのよてい  *watashi no yotei*  → my plan
2. わたしのよていも  *watashi no yotei mo*  → my plan, too
3. わたしのよていもえいがです。  *watashi no yotei mo eiga desu.*  → My plan is a movie too.

1. ゆき  *Yuki*  → Yuki
2. ゆきも  *Yuki mo*  → Yuki too
3. ゆきもやすみです。  *Yuki mo yasumi desu.*  → Yuki is off too.
4. ゆきもやすみですか。  *Yuki mo yasumi desu ka.*  → Are you off too, Yuki?

> **Tip:** も replaces は. Don't say both (no 'わたしはも'). Think of も as a 'me-too' sticker you put on the new topic, and the rest of the sentence repeats what was already said.

### Real Japanese (TV weather report)

**あしたの東京も雨です。**  
Tomorrow, Tokyo will have rain too.

Until now you've used は to mark the topic: 私は がくせいです (As for me, I'm a student). When you want to say 'X too' or 'X also', you swap は out for も. The rest of the sentence stays exactly the same, word for word: topic, then the particle, then the noun, then です. So 私は やすみです (I'm off) becomes 私も やすみです (I'm off too). も does the 'too' job and the topic job together, so you never write は and も side by side.

- Slot order doesn't change: [Topic] + も + [Noun] + です. Only the particle after the topic changes, from は to も.
- も goes right after the thing that is 'also' true. In 私の よていも えいがです, the 'also' belongs to 私の よてい (my plan), so も comes after that whole phrase.
- Never use は and も together. も replaces は completely. 私はも is wrong.
- To ask a question, add か at the end the usual way: ゆきも やすみですか。= Are you off too, Yuki?
- In English, 'too' comes at the end of the sentence, but in Japanese も comes right after the word it's about, near the start of the sentence.

*This is the same 〜は〜です frame from episode 1, with も in the topic marker's place. It also works with の phrases from earlier episodes: 私の よていも… attaches も to the whole 'my plan' chunk, just as は would.*

### Swap-in examples

1. 私(わたし)もかいしゃいんです。  
   *Watashi mo kaishain desu.*  
   I'm an office worker too.  (swap → base sentence: 私も [noun] です)

2. けんもかいしゃいんです。  
   *Ken mo kaishain desu.*  
   Ken is an office worker too.  (swap → topic: 私 → けん)

3. けんもアメリカ人(じん)ですか。  
   *Ken mo amerikajin desu ka.*  
   Is Ken American too?  (swap → noun: かいしゃいん → アメリカ人 (+ か to make a question))

4. あしたもやすみです。  
   *Ashita mo yasumi desu.*  
   Tomorrow is a day off too.  (swap → topic: けん → あした; noun: → やすみ)

5. にちようびのよていもえいがです。  
   *Nichiyoubi no yotei mo eiga desu.*  
   Sunday's plan is a movie too.  (swap → topic: あした → にちようびの よてい (a の phrase); noun: → えいが)

## Dialogue

**1. Yuki:** けん、しゅうまつはやすみですか。  
*Ken, shuumatsu wa yasumi desu ka.*  
Ken, are you off this weekend?  
Literal: `Ken, weekend TOPIC day-off is QUESTION`

| けん | しゅうまつ | は | やすみ | です | か |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ken | shuumatsu | wa | yasumi | desu | ka |
| *Ken* | *weekend* | *topic marker* | *day off* | *is* | *question marker* |

**2. Ken:** はい、やすみです。ゆきもやすみですか。  
*Hai, yasumi desu. Yuki mo yasumi desu ka.*  
Yes, I'm off. Are you off too, Yuki?  
Literal: `yes, day-off is. Yuki ALSO day-off is QUESTION`

| はい | やすみ | です | ゆき | も | やすみ | です | か |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| hai | yasumi | desu | yuki | mo | yasumi | desu | ka |

**3. Yuki:** ええ、私(わたし)もやすみです。どようびのよていは何(なん)ですか。  
*Ee, watashi mo yasumi desu. Doyoubi no yotei wa nan desu ka.*

Literal: `yeah, I ALSO day-off am. Saturday OF plan TOPIC what is QUESTION`

## Teacher script

Welcome back! Today's pattern is **AもBです**. It means: A is also B. Or: A is B, too.  
The only new piece is one tiny word: **も**. It means also, or too.  
> 🔊 わたしもやすみです。  
That means: I'm off too.  
Here are the slots. A is the topic: a person, a thing, a day. B is the noun you're saying about it. And **も** sits exactly where **は** used to sit.  
So **も** doesn't get added to the sentence. It replaces **は**. Topic, then **も**, then the noun, then **です** last, as always.  
Notice the word order. In English, also floats in the middle: I am also off. In Japanese, **も** sticks right onto the thing that's also true: I, too, day off, is.  
Let's build one. Start with the topic **わたし**, meaning I.  
> 🔊 わたし  
Add **も**. Now it's: me too.  
> 🔊 わたしも  
Add **やすみ**, a day off, and finish with **です**.  
> 🔊 わたしもやすみです。  
I'm off too. Yuki says exactly this in today's dialogue.  
Now a longer topic. Start with **わたしのよてい**: my plan.  
> 🔊 わたしのよてい  
Add **も**: my plan, too.  
> 🔊 わたしのよていも  
Then **えいが**, a movie, and **です**.  
> 🔊 わたしのよていもえいがです。  
My plan is a movie too. Everything before **も** is the topic, so it's the plan that's also a movie.  
Third, let's make a question. Start with a name.  
> 🔊 ゆき  
Add **も**: Yuki too.  
> 🔊 ゆきも  
Add the noun and **です**.  
> 🔊 ゆきもやすみです。  
Yuki is off too. Now add **か** at the very end.  
> 🔊 ゆきもやすみですか。  
Are you off too, Yuki? That's Ken's line.  
Same skeleton, new words. Listen to Ken:  
> 🔊 あさごはんのみせもえきです。  
My breakfast place is at the station too. Topic: the breakfast place. Then **も**. Then **えき**, station. Then **です**.  
> 🔊 にちようびもやすみです。  
Sunday is a day off too. Only the topic changed. The pattern stays the same.  
The most common beginner mistake is putting **は** and **も** together. Don't stack them. **も** takes **は**'s seat.  
Memory trick: think of **も** as a little me-too sticker. You stick it on the new topic, and the rest of the sentence repeats what you already heard.  
This snaps onto what you already know. Put it with **じゃないです** and it means: not either.  
> 🔊 わたしもやすみじゃないです。  
I'm not off either. And **の** still builds the topic, like **ともだちのみせ**, a friend's restaurant.
