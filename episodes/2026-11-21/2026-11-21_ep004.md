# Episode 4: 私の コーヒーですか — Whose Coffee Is This?
*2026-11-21 · Topic: at a restaurant · Audio: `2026-11-21_ep004.mp3`*

## Today's pattern

### AのB  ·  *A no B*  =  **A's B / the B of A**

- **A**: The owner or describer: a person (わたし, けん, ゆき, ともだち), a place (みせ, とうきょう), or a time (あした). It always comes first.
- **B**: The main thing you are talking about: an object, a person, or a place (コーヒー, ケーキ, メニュー, みせ, ともだち).

**〜の〜** — の links nouns (my, of, 's)

Structure: `[Noun A] の [Noun B]`

### Building practical sentences

1. わたし  *watashi*  → I / me
2. わたしの  *watashi no*  → my
3. わたしのコーヒー  *watashi no koohii*  → my coffee
4. これはわたしのコーヒーです。  *Kore wa watashi no koohii desu.*  → This is my coffee.
5. これはわたしのコーヒーですか。  *Kore wa watashi no koohii desu ka.*  → Is this my coffee?

1. ともだちの  *tomodachi no*  → friend's
2. ともだちのみせ  *tomodachi no mise*  → a friend's restaurant
3. わたしのともだちのみせ  *watashi no tomodachi no mise*  → my friend's restaurant
4. これはわたしのともだちのみせです。  *Kore wa watashi no tomodachi no mise desu.*  → This is my friend's restaurant.

1. けんの  *Ken no*  → Ken's
2. けんのケーキです。  *Ken no keeki desu.*  → It's Ken's cake. (It's your cake, Ken.)

> **Tip:** Owner first, always. の works like English apostrophe-s: whatever comes before の owns or describes whatever comes after it. けんのケーキ is Ken's cake. Flip it to ケーキのけん and you've said 'the cake's Ken'. When English uses 'of' (the menu of the restaurant), turn it around in Japanese: みせのメニュー.

### Real Japanese (TV weather report)

**あしたのとうきょうのてんきです。**  
Here is tomorrow's weather for Tokyo. (Literally: It's tomorrow's Tokyo's weather.)

の is a small glue word that joins two nouns. The noun before の is the owner or the describer, and the noun after の is the actual thing. So 私の コーヒー is literally 'I's coffee', meaning 'my coffee'. English uses 'my', 'your', ''s' or 'of', but Japanese uses the same pattern every time: [A] の [B]. The whole group 'A の B' then works like one big noun, so you can put it anywhere a single noun can go, such as before です or before は.

- Order: owner first, thing second. ゆきの ともだち = Yuki's friend (the friend belongs to Yuki). If you swap them, the meaning changes.
- The main noun always comes last. In みせの メニュー the thing is the menu, and みせ (restaurant) tells you which menu.
- Use 私の for 'my', and a name + の for 'your' or 'his/her': けんの ケーキ = Ken's cake. Japanese often uses the person's name where English says 'your'.
- You can chain の: 私の ともだちの みせ = my friend's restaurant. Read it left to right: I → friend → restaurant.
- A の B is one noun block, so it fits the slots you already know: これは [A の B] です。 / [A の B] は [Noun] です。 / これは [A の B] じゃないです。

*In 〜は〜です, 〜ですか and 〜じゃないです every slot held one noun. Now any of those nouns can grow into a bigger 'A の B' block, and the rest of the sentence stays exactly the same.*

### Swap-in examples

1. これは私(わたし)のおちゃです。  
   *Kore wa watashi no ocha desu.*  
   This is my tea.  (swap → base sentence: 私の おちゃ)

2. これはゆきのおちゃです。  
   *Kore wa Yuki no ocha desu.*  
   This is Yuki's tea.  (swap → owner: 私 → ゆき)

3. これはゆきのケーキですか。  
   *Kore wa Yuki no keeki desu ka.*  
   Is this Yuki's cake?  (swap → thing: おちゃ → ケーキ, made into a question with か)

4. けんのともだちはアメリカ人(じん)です。  
   *Ken no tomodachi wa amerikajin desu.*  
   Ken's friend is American.  (swap → the A の B block moves into the topic slot: けんの ともだち は ...)

5. けんのともだちは学生(がくせい)じゃないです。  
   *Ken no tomodachi wa gakusei janaidesu.*  
   Ken's friend is not a student.  (swap → description: アメリカ人です → 学生じゃないです)

## Teacher script

Welcome back! Today's pattern is **AのB**. It means: A's B, or the B of A. The little word **の** links two nouns together.  
Here's the whole idea in one short phrase.  
> 🔊 わたしのコーヒー  
My coffee. Word for word, that's: me, link word, coffee. Me-'s coffee.  
Let's walk through the slots. Slot A is the owner, or whatever describes the thing: a person, a place, a group. Slot B is the main thing you're actually talking about.  
And **の** sits right between them, doing the job of apostrophe-s in English.  
> 🔊 けんのケーキ  
Ken's cake. Good news: that's the same order as English. Owner first, then the thing. But when English says 'the menu of the restaurant', Japanese flips it around and puts the restaurant first.  
> 🔊 みせのメニュー  
The restaurant's menu. Restaurant, or shop, is **みせ**. Menu is **メニュー**.  
Now let's build a full sentence. Start with the owner, which is me.  
> 🔊 わたし  
Add the link word, and 'me' becomes 'my'.  
> 🔊 わたしの  
Now add the thing, coffee.  
> 🔊 わたしのコーヒー  
My coffee. Now drop it into a frame you already know: this, **は**, then the noun, then **です**.  
> 🔊 これはわたしのコーヒーです。  
This is my coffee. Add **か** at the end and it becomes a question.  
> 🔊 これはわたしのコーヒーですか。  
Is this my coffee? Ken asks exactly that in today's dialogue.  
You can also chain **の** more than once. Start with friend, **ともだち**, plus the link word.  
> 🔊 ともだちの  
Friend's. Add restaurant.  
> 🔊 ともだちのみせ  
A friend's restaurant. Now put 'my' in front.  
> 🔊 わたしのともだちのみせ  
My friend's restaurant. Wrap it in the sentence frame.  
> 🔊 これはわたしのともだちのみせです。  
This is my friend's restaurant. One more, a quick one. Ken's.  
> 🔊 けんの  
Add cake and **です**.  
> 🔊 けんのケーキです。  
It's Ken's cake. Notice that Yuki uses Ken's name where English would say 'your'. Japanese does that a lot.  
Now a swap. Same skeleton, different words. Instead of my coffee, my tea, which is **おちゃ**.  
> 🔊 わたしのおちゃです。  
It's my tea. And instead of my friend, Yuki's friend.  
> 🔊 ゆきのともだちはせんせいですか。  
Is Yuki's friend a teacher? Same **の**, brand new sentence.  
Here's the common mistake: getting the order backwards. The owner always goes before **の**. If you said this...  
> 🔊 ケーキのけん  
...you'd be saying 'the cake's Ken', a Ken who belongs to the cake! Memory trick: **の** is apostrophe-s. Whatever comes before it owns whatever comes after it.  
And here's how it fits with what you already know. A whole **の** phrase works like one big noun, so it goes anywhere a noun goes: before **です**, before **ですか**, and before **じゃないです**.  
> 🔊 わたしのコーヒーじゃないです。  
It's not my coffee. Now let's hear Yuki and Ken use it.
