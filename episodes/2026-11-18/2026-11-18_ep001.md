# Episode 1: 私はコーヒーです — I'll Have Coffee
*2026-11-18 · Topic: ordering coffee at a café · Audio: `2026-11-18_ep001.mp3`*

## Today's pattern

### AはBです  ·  *A wa B desu*  =  **A is B (literally: as for A, B is it)**

- **A**: The topic: a person, a thing, or a place. For example 私 (I), これ (this), 東京 (Tokyo), a person's name.
- **B**: A noun that says what the topic is, or what goes with it. For example おちゃ (tea), コーヒー (coffee), a name, the weather (晴れ, sunny).

**〜は〜です** — X is Y (topic は + noun + です)

Structure: `[Topic] は [Noun] です。`

### Building practical sentences

1. これ  *kore*  → this
2. これは  *kore wa*  → as for this
3. これはおちゃです。  *kore wa ocha desu.*  → This is tea.

1. 私  *watashi*  → I
2. 私は  *watashi wa*  → as for me
3. 私はけんです。  *watashi wa Ken desu.*  → I am Ken.

1. 私は  *watashi wa*  → as for me
2. 私はコーヒー  *watashi wa koohii*  → as for me, coffee
3. 私はコーヒーです。  *watashi wa koohii desu.*  → I'll have coffee. (literally: as for me, it's coffee)

> **Tip:** は (wa) means "as for" and marks the topic. It doesn't mean "equals." So 私はコーヒーです (watashi wa koohii desu) means "As for me, coffee," which is how you say "I'll have coffee." It does not mean "I am coffee." Memory trick: です (desu) is like a full stop. It always comes last and closes the sentence.

### Real Japanese (TV weather report)

**今日、東京は晴れです。**  
Today, it's sunny in Tokyo. (literally: today, as for Tokyo, it's sunny)

This sentence pattern has three slots: [Topic] は [Noun] です. The first slot is the topic, meaning the thing you are talking about. The small word は comes right after it and works like a label that says "this is my topic." The last slot is a noun followed by です, which roughly means "is" or "am" and makes the sentence polite. So これはコーヒーです is built as "this (topic) / coffee / is", which means "This is coffee."

- Word order is fixed: topic first, then は, then the noun, then です at the very end. In Japanese, the 'is' word comes last, not in the middle as in English.
- は is a particle, a small word that marks the role of the word in front of it. It always comes AFTER the word it marks. Here it marks the topic. It is written は but pronounced 'wa'.
- です does not change for I, you, he, or it. 私はコーヒーです, けんはコーヒーです, and これはコーヒーです all end with the same です.
- は means 'as for X', not strictly 'X equals Y'. That's why 私はコーヒーです (as for me: coffee) is a normal way to order at a café. It means 'I'll have coffee', not 'I am a coffee'.
- Japanese has no words for 'a' or 'the', so コーヒー can mean 'coffee', 'a coffee', or 'the coffee', depending on context.

*This is the first pattern, so there is nothing earlier to link it to. But you have already heard です inside the set phrase そうですか: it is the same です you just learned, with a question marker added (you'll learn that later).*

### Swap-in examples

1. 私(わたし)はおちゃです。  
   *Watashi wa ocha desu.*  
   I'll have tea.  (swap → noun: コーヒー → おちゃ)

2. けんはコーヒーです。  
   *Ken wa koohii desu.*  
   Ken is having coffee.  (swap → topic: 私 → けん)

3. ゆきはおちゃです。  
   *Yuki wa ocha desu.*  
   Yuki is having tea.  (swap → topic: けん → ゆき, noun: コーヒー → おちゃ)

4. これはコーヒーです。  
   *Kore wa koohii desu.*  
   This is coffee.  (swap → topic: ゆき → これ)

## Teacher script

Welcome to your very first episode! Today we learn the most useful sentence shape in Japanese. Here it is.  
> 🔊 AはBです  
That means: A is B. Something, then **は**, then something else, then **です**.  
Let's take it apart. First comes A, the topic. That's the thing you want to talk about: a person, a thing, a place.  
Right after the topic comes the little word **は**. It flags the topic. Think of it as 'as for.' As for this. As for me.  
Next comes B, a noun that tells us about the topic. And at the very end we say **です**. That's the 'is' part, and it also makes the sentence polite.  
Here's the big difference from English. In English, 'is' sits in the middle: this is tea. In Japanese, **です** waits until the very end: this, tea, is.  
Let's build one together. Start with the word for 'this.'  
> 🔊 これ  
'This.' Now add the topic marker.  
> 🔊 これは  
'As for this.' Now add the word for green tea, **おちゃ**, and finish with **です**.  
> 🔊 これはおちゃです。  
'This is tea.' Your first full Japanese sentence!  
Let's build another one. Start with the word for 'I.'  
> 🔊 私  
'I.' Add the topic marker.  
> 🔊 私は  
'As for me.' Now add Ken's name, and finish with **です**.  
> 🔊 私はけんです。  
'I am Ken.' That's how you introduce yourself.  
Now one more, a really handy one. Same start, as for me.  
> 🔊 私は  
Now add the word for coffee, **コーヒー**, and finish.  
> 🔊 私はコーヒーです。  
Word for word, that's 'as for me, coffee.' In a café it means 'I'll have coffee.'  
Now watch how the frame stays the same while the words change. Swap tea for coffee.  
> 🔊 これはコーヒーです。  
'This is coffee.' Or swap the name.  
> 🔊 私はゆきです。  
'I am Yuki.' Same frame, new words. That's what makes it a pattern.  
Now a common beginner mistake. When you hear Ken order coffee, don't panic and think he's saying 'I am coffee.'  
Remember that **は** means 'as for.' So the sentence just sets up a topic and then adds something about it. As for me, coffee. Japanese leaves the rest to context.  
And one memory trick: **です** works like a full stop. When you hear it, the sentence is finished.  
Well done. Everything else you learn will be built on this pattern, so you've just laid the foundation.  
Now let's hear Yuki and Ken use it. Listen for **は** and **です**, and see if one of them gets the drinks mixed up.
