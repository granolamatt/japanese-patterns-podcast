#1 · 2026-11-18 · episode
AはBです A wa B desu
= A is B (literally: as for A, B is it)

A = The topic: a person, a thing, or a place. For example 私 (I), これ (this), 東京 (Tokyo), a person's name.; B = A noun that says what the topic is, or what goes with it. For example おちゃ (tea), コーヒー (coffee), a name, the weather (晴れ, sunny).

私(わたし)はおちゃです。
Watashi wa ocha desu.
I'll have tea.

💡 は (wa) means "as for" and marks the topic. It doesn't mean "equals." So 私はコーヒーです (watashi wa koohii desu) means "As for me, coffee," which is how you say "I'll have coffee." It does not mean "I am coffee." Memory trick: です (desu) is like a full stop. It always comes last and closes the sentence.

#2 · 2026-11-19 · episode
AはBですか A wa B desu ka
= Is A B?

A = the topic: a thing, a person, a time word like today or tomorrow, or 'this' (これ); B = a noun you're asking about: weather (あめ, はれ, くもり), a drink (コーヒー, おちゃ), a person's name or job

今日(きょう)はあめですか。
Kyou wa ame desu ka.
Is it rainy today?

💡 か is a question mark you can hear. You don't change the word order or add a separate word for 'is'. Just take any AはBです sentence and put か at the end. To answer, start with はい (yes) or いいえ (no).

#3 · 2026-11-20 · episode
AはBじゃないです A wa B ja nai desu
= A is not B

A = the topic: a person (私, a name like けん), a thing (これ), or a time or place (あした, きょう); B = a noun that A is NOT: a job (学生, かいしゃいん, せんせい), a nationality (日本人), a thing (コーヒー), or the weather (雨)

私(わたし)は学生(がくせい)じゃないです。
Watashi wa gakusei janaidesu.
I'm not a student.

💡 The negative ending じゃないです replaces です. It doesn't come after it, so never say "desu ja nai desu." And because "not" comes at the very end in Japanese, always listen to the last word to know whether a sentence is yes or no.

#4 · 2026-11-21 · episode
AのB A no B
= A's B / the B of A

A = The owner or describer: a person (わたし, けん, ゆき, ともだち), a place (みせ, とうきょう), or a time (あした). It always comes first.; B = The main thing you are talking about: an object, a person, or a place (コーヒー, ケーキ, メニュー, みせ, ともだち).

これは私(わたし)のおちゃです。
Kore wa watashi no ocha desu.
This is my tea.

💡 Owner first, always. の works like English apostrophe-s: whatever comes before の owns or describes whatever comes after it. けんのケーキ is Ken's cake. Flip it to ケーキのけん and you've said 'the cake's Ken'. When English uses 'of' (the menu of the restaurant), turn it around in Japanese: みせのメニュー.

#5 · 2026-11-22 · episode
これはAです／このAはBです kore wa A desu / kono A wa B desu
= This is A / This A is B

これ／それ／あれ = これ = this (near me), それ = that (near you), あれ = that over there (far from both of us). They stand alone as the topic, with no noun after them.; この／その／あの = The same three distances, but they must come right before a noun: このパン (this bread), そのかさ (that umbrella), あのケーキ (that cake over there).; A = A thing: food, a drink, an object (おちゃ, パン, かさ, ケーキ); B = A description noun, often with の: みせのパン, 私のかさ, ともだちのケーキ

これは私(わたし)のコーヒーです。
Kore wa watashi no koohii desu.
This is my coffee.

💡 これ／それ／あれ stand alone, but この／その／あの always need a noun right after them. Never say これパン. Say このパン, or これはパンです. Memory trick: the の in この is like the linker の, and it always needs a noun after it.

#6 · 2026-11-23 · episode
Aは なん／どこ／だれ ですか A wa nan / doko / dare desu ka
= What / where / who is A?

A = the topic you're asking about: a thing, place, or person (トイレ, あれ, このかさ, えき); question word = なん (what), どこ (where), or だれ (who). It goes exactly where the answer will go. Add の for 'whose' (だれの) or 'what kind of' (なんの)

えきはどこですか。
Eki wa doko desu ka.
Where is the station?

💡 Don't move the question word to the front like in English. Put it right where the answer will go: トイレはどこですか → トイレはあれです. Also, "what" is なに, but before です it becomes なん: say なんですか, never なにですか.

#7 · 2026-11-24 · episode
AもBです A mo B desu
= A is also B / A is B, too

A = The topic that is 'also' true: a person (わたし, ゆき), a thing, a day (にちようび), or a の-chunk like わたしのよてい. も replaces は here.; B = A noun that was already said about something else: a day off (やすみ), a movie (えいが), a place (えき).

私(わたし)もかいしゃいんです。
Watashi mo kaishain desu.
I'm an office worker too.

💡 も replaces は. Don't say both (no 'わたしはも'). Think of も as a 'me-too' sticker you put on the new topic, and the rest of the sentence repeats what was already said.

#8 · 2026-11-25 · episode
AはBです（B＝い形容詞） A wa B desu (B = an i-adjective)
= A is B (B = a describing word like cute, expensive, cheap)

A = The topic: a thing, person or place, e.g. これ, それ, このシャツ; B = An い-adjective, a describing word that ends in い: かわいい (cute), たかい (expensive), やすい (cheap), さむい (cold)

このシャツはやすいです。
Kono shatsu wa yasui desu.
This shirt is cheap.

💡 The final い is part of an い-adjective, so never drop it: たかいです, not たかです. Think of い as the adjective's little tail that always stays on. Also remember word order: the describing word comes first and です always comes last.

#9 · 2026-11-26 · episode
AはBくないです A wa B-kunai desu
= A is not B

A = the topic: a thing, place, food or drink, often with この/その/あれ (このおにぎり, このコーヒー, 東京); B = an い-adjective with its final い removed (たか-, あつ-, おいし-, つめた-). Exception: いい becomes よくない

このケーキはたかくないです。
Kono keeki wa takakunai desu.
This cake is not expensive.

💡 Don't put じゃないです after an い-adjective (たかいじゃないです is wrong). Nouns take じゃない; い-adjectives swap their final い for くない: たかい → たかくないです. Exception: いい (good) → よくないです.

#10 · 2026-11-27 · episode
AはBです／AはBじゃないです A wa B desu / A wa B ja nai desu
= A is B / A isn't B (B = a na-adjective)

A = the topic: a person, thing or place (おかあさん, 私のかぞく, このへや), often built with の or この; B = a な-adjective describing A: きれい (pretty / clean), げんき (energetic, well), しずか (quiet), ゆうめい (famous), ふあんてい (unstable). Used plainly, with no な before です

おとうさんはげんきです。
Otousan wa genki desu.
My father is doing well.

💡 きれい ends in an い sound, but it's a な-adjective, so its negative is きれいじゃないです, never きれいくないです. A memory trick: な-adjectives act like nouns (add です or じゃないです), and い-adjectives change their own ending (〜くないです).

#11 · 2026-11-28 · episode
A N ／ B な N A N / B na N
= an A N / a B N (the adjective comes before the noun, just like in English)

A = an い-adjective in its dictionary form, attached directly to the noun: おいしい, あつい, つめたい, たのしい, おおきい; B = a な-adjective followed by な: しずか, きれい, ゆうめい, げんき; N = any noun: コーヒー, ケーキ, みせ, やすみ, まち, よてい

これはおいしいコーヒーです。
Kore wa oishii koohii desu.
This is good coffee.

💡 Never put な after an い-adjective: it's あつい コーヒー, not あついな コーヒー. Memory trick: い-adjectives stand on their own; な-adjectives need な to hold the noun's hand. And な only appears in front of a noun. Before です, it's just しずかです.
